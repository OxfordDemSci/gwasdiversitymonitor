Full counting gives each linked funder one complete publication credit. For example, if a publication lists three funders in PubMed's GrantList, it counts once for each funder—not one third each as fractional counting would. Every GWAS Catalog study accession from that publication is included in each linked funder's filtered view, so funder totals overlap and must not be added together.

Full counting gives each named cohort one complete credit for every GWAS Catalog study accession linked to it. If an accession names three cohorts, that accession—and its full recorded participant and ancestry profile—appears in each cohort's filtered view, rather than contributing one third to each under fractional counting. Cohort totals therefore overlap and are not additive; they describe published GWAS linked to a cohort, not the cohort's overall demographics.
